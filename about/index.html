About Nitya Bhakti

नित्य (nitya) means daily, constant, eternal. The idea is simple: a small, beautiful place to read the Gita and chant, every day, without noise.

Why this exists

Most devotional software is cluttered with ads, pop-ups and paywalls. Nitya Bhakti is built the other way: a calm reading room for the Bhagavad Gita, a chanting mala, and the mantras people actually keep by heart. It loads fast, works offline, and asks for nothing.

Sources

The Sanskrit text of the Bhagavad Gita is in the public domain. English translations are by Swami Sivananda; Hindi translations by Swami Ramsukhdas (Gita Press tradition); both via the open dataset published by the Gita Initiative. Mantras are cited with their scriptural source on each card. Nitya Bhakti is a study aid made with respect for the tradition — for learning, sit with a teacher.

Privacy

There are no accounts, no analytics, and no tracking. Your mala count, rounds and streaks are stored only in your own browser on your device. Add Nitya Bhakti to your home screen and it works fully offline.

Adding it to your phone

Open Nitya Bhakti in your browser, then choose “Add to Home Screen”. It installs like an app, keeps your practice on the device, and needs no app store.
