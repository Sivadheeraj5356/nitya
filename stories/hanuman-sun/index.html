The child Hanuman leaps for the sun

A story of Hanuman · retold from Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda (traditional telling)

Hanuman murti at Parmarth Ashram, Rishikesh - photo: Deepak Naik, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

When Hanuman was a child, he woke one morning hungry and saw the rising sun - red and golden as a ripe fruit. Wanting to taste it, he simply leapt into the sky after it.

Indra, alarmed at this little being streaking toward the sun, struck him down with his thunderbolt. The child fell to earth, his jaw - hanu - bruised, giving him the name Hanuman. But then his father Vayu, the wind god, in grief and anger, withdrew all air from the world, and every creature began to suffocate.

The gods came at once to make peace. They restored the child, and each gave him a blessing: strength beyond measure, wisdom, protection from weapons and fire and water. The boy who leapt for the sun grew into the one who would leap across the ocean - proof that what looks like reckless daring, when joined to devotion, becomes the greatest service.

Retold in our own words from Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda (traditional telling). Translations and tellings vary across traditions; we follow the best-known account.
