Rama accepts fourteen years of exile

A story of Rama · retold from Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda

Rama Darbar with Sita, Lakshmana, Bharata, Shatrughna and Hanuman, Ravi Varma Press oleograph · public domain

On the morning Rama was to be crowned king of Ayodhya, his stepmother Kaikeyi called in two old boons owed to her by the king: her own son Bharata would be crowned, and Rama would go into the forest for fourteen years.

Rama was washing for the coronation when the summons came. He heard the words, and his face did not change. He did not argue that the promise was not his. He did not curse Kaikeyi. He touched his weeping father's feet, comforted his mother, and went to lay aside his royal robes for bark cloth.

His wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana refused to stay behind - where Rama goes, they said, is our kingdom. The whole city wept as the three walked out of the gates. The Ramayana says the measure of a person is not how they wear a crown, but how they lose one. Rama kept his father's word, and fourteen years later, he came home.

Retold in our own words from Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda. Translations and tellings vary across traditions; we follow the best-known account.
