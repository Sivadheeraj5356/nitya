Ganesha writes the Mahabharata

A story of Ganesha · retold from Mahabharata, Adi Parva (traditional account)

Ganesha idol at Maa Durga Sai Baba Temple, Orlando - photo: Kiran891, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The sage Vyasa had composed the Mahabharata in his mind - a hundred thousand verses - and needed a scribe equal to the task. Ganesha agreed, on one condition: Vyasa must dictate without pausing. Vyasa made his own condition in return: Ganesha must understand every verse before writing it down.

So the greatest epic ever told flowed between them. When Vyasa needed rest, he would speak a verse so deep and knotty that even Ganesha had to stop and think - and in that pause, the sage caught his breath.

When Ganesha's quill broke mid-verse, he did not stop. He broke off a piece of his own tusk and kept writing, so the work would not be interrupted. That is why one of his tusks is short, and why he is invoked before every beginning: the remover of obstacles who gives of himself to finish what matters.

Retold in our own words from Mahabharata, Adi Parva (traditional account). Translations and tellings vary across traditions; we follow the best-known account.
