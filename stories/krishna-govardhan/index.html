Krishna lifts Govardhan Hill

A story of Krishna · retold from Bhagavata Purana, Book 10

Yashoda with Krishna, Raja Ravi Varma · public domain

The people of Vrindavan prepared their yearly offering to Indra, king of the heavens. Young Krishna asked his father: why do we worship a distant god for the rain, when it is the hill, the cows and the fields that feed us? So the villagers offered their food to Govardhan Hill instead.

Indra's pride was wounded. He sent a storm such as no one had ever seen - black clouds, thunder, rain like falling rivers. The people ran to Krishna, and Krishna did what no one else could: he lifted the whole of Govardhan Hill on the little finger of his left hand, and held it like an umbrella over every person, cow and calf.

For seven days the storm raged and failed. Then Indra understood who stood before him, and the rain stopped. The story is told to children across India to this day: when pride attacks, shelter is found not in power, but in love.

Retold in our own words from Bhagavata Purana, Book 10. Translations and tellings vary across traditions; we follow the best-known account.
