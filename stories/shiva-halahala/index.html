Shiva drinks the poison

A story of Shiva · retold from Bhagavata Purana, Book 8; Ramayana, Balakanda

Shiva statue at Kempfort, Bengaluru - photo: Kalyan Kumar, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

To win the nectar of immortality, the gods and demons churned the ocean of milk together, with a mountain as the churn and the serpent Vasuki as the rope. Treasures rose from the depths - but before the nectar came something else: the Halahala, a poison so fierce its fumes began to scorch all three worlds.

Gods and demons alike fled in terror. Only Shiva stepped forward. He gathered the poison in his palm and drank it, holding it in his throat so it could not reach his body or the worlds within him. The poison burned his throat blue, and so one of his beloved names is Neelakantha - the blue-throated one.

The churning went on, and the nectar rose at last. The story is a quiet teaching: the truly strong do not run from what is toxic in the world. They contain it, so that others may live.

Retold in our own words from Bhagavata Purana, Book 8; Ramayana, Balakanda. Translations and tellings vary across traditions; we follow the best-known account.
